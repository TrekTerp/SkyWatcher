Skywatcher

Is it worth going out tonight? A single-file planning dashboard for amateur observers.

A self-contained, single-file astronomy planning dashboard for amateur observers. No server, no account, no internet connection required — open the HTML file in any modern browser.

Fully configurable for any location via ZIP code or the ⌖ Locate button. Default location is San Francisco CA.

Quick Start

1. Open index.html in Chrome, Firefox, or Safari

2. Optionally enter your ZIP code in Settings and click Apply, or click ⌖ Locate to use your browser's geolocation

3. Use the five tabs to explore the year, plan a specific night, or check Jupiter, Saturn, and deep sky objects

Your location is saved in the URL hash (e.g. #94102 or #37.77,-122.41) so it persists across reloads and can be bookmarked or shared.

Tabs

Year View

An annual Gantt-style timeline showing every tracked object's visibility across the full calendar year. Each row is one object; bars represent nights when the object rises above your effective horizon during your observing window.

Bar encoding:

  • Bar length = observable season
  • Bar opacity = peak altitude quality (brighter = higher culmination)
  • Hover Moon, Venus and Mercury bars for phase and illumination at the hovered date

Overlaid event markers (each toggleable via checkboxes):

An altitude-vs-time chart for any chosen date, covering dusk through dawn. Each tracked object gets a curve showing its altitude through the night.

Jupiter overlay (when Jupiter is visible):

  • Orange stripe on Jupiter's curve = Great Red Spot within ±35° of central meridian
  • Silver stripe = Galilean moon shadow transit in progress, labeled with moon initial (I/E/G/C)

  • Bracket markers with separation label when two objects come within 10° of each other during the night

  • Date picker accepts any date (defaults to tonight's local date)

Jupiter Tab

A monthly event timeline for the Galilean moon system, designed to identify high-value observing nights.

Event rows: Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto (shadow transits and disk transits), plus GRS (Great Red Spot).

Visual hierarchy:

  • Shadow transits: full-height silver/white bars (highest priority)
  • Moon transits: shorter, dimmer bars
  • GRS: orange bars when within ±35° of central meridian
  • Rare overlaps (double shadow, shadow + GRS): pulsing gold highlight

Best Nights chips — scored and ranked by shadow transit (+3), GRS (+2), rare overlap (+4). Top 8 shown; click any chip to jump to Single Night.

Column date alignment: All bars display under their local evening date (not UT date). Events in the early morning UT hours correctly appear under the previous local date.

Callisto transit gap note: When Callisto transits are geometrically impossible (B₀ too large), an info banner explains why and gives the next expected window.

Navigation: ‹ Month Year › arrows, right-click any bar to jump to Single Night.

GRS longitude: filled automatically for the displayed month from a drift model (System II 91° on 1 Jun 2026, +1.75°/month, per Sky & Telescope / JUPOS). Type a value and Apply to override for the session (the field turns gold); Auto returns to the model.

Astronomical Methods

Coordinate System

Planets are computed in the J2000 ecliptic (Earth in the same frame), rotated to J2000 equatorial, then precessed to the mean equator and equinox of date. The Sun and Moon are computed of date directly, and deep sky catalog positions (J2000) are precessed to date. Local sidereal time then gives the hour angle for altitude and azimuth.

Core Ephemerides

Sun — Meeus Ch.25. Mean longitude + equation of center. ~0.01° accuracy.

Moon — Meeus Ch.47 with the leading terms of Tables 47.A/B (32 longitude/distance terms, 20 latitude terms, plus the additive terms). Typically under 1′. Altitudes, rise/set and occultations use the topocentric Moon (parallax up to ~1°).

Planets — JPL "Keplerian Elements for Approximate Positions of the Major Planets" (Standish, 1800–2050 table) with an iterative Kepler solution and one light-time iteration.

Rise/set/transit — sampled altitude with linear interpolation; ~1 minute.

Measured accuracy (v13)

Jupiter — Galilean Moon System

Algorithm: Meeus Ch.44 low-accuracy method — mean longitudes relative to the Earth–Jupiter line with light-time (Δ/173 d), the ψ − B geometry terms, the main mutual perturbations, and the Jovicentric declination of Earth (DE) for the north–south tilt.


x =  r · sin(u)                 [+ = west]
y = −r · cos(u) · sin(D_E)      [+ = north]
z = −r · cos(u)                 [z < 0 = in front of Jupiter]

Transit detection: z < 0 AND x² + (y/0.935)² < 1

Shadows: shadow_x = x + s · r, where s = ±sin(phase angle at Jupiter) is computed at each instant (+ before opposition, − after).

Measured accuracy: against JPL Horizons (Oct 2026) the moon positions match to 0.01–0.03 Rj, equivalent to timing offsets of +1.5 min (Io), +0.5 min (Europa), +3 min (Ganymede), +5.5 min (Callisto). Against published January 2026 event tables, transit and shadow start/end times agree within 0–2 minutes. Matches Meeus Example 44.a to 0.02 Rj.

GRS: System II central meridian from Meeus Ch.43 (low-accuracy method) with light-time correction: CM_II = 187.23° + 870.1869088° · (d − Δ/173) + ψ − B, d = JD − 2451545.0. Matches Meeus's worked example to ~1° (≈2 min). The GRS counts as visible within ±35° of the central meridian; the GRS longitude itself comes from the S&T/JUPOS drift model and is the main uncertainty.

Callisto transit seasons: possible only when |DE| < 2.03°. Current season ~mid-2025 to ~Sep 2028; next from ~Dec 2031. The Jupiter tab computes these dates.

Saturn — Ring System

Ring tilt B — Meeus Ch.45:


B = arcsin(sin(28.048°) · cos(β) · sin(λ − 169.53°) − cos(28.048°) · sin(β))

B > 0 = north face toward Earth. 2026: south face, rings opening after the March 2025 edge-on crossing.

Titan transits — possible only when |B| < 2.83° (= arcsin(1/20.27 Rs)). Last window: mid-2024 through early Feb 2026. Next: ~2038–2040.

Eclipses and Occultations

Lunar eclipses — Moon–antisun separation at full moon; umbral magnitude from shadow geometry.

Solar eclipses — Moon–Sun separation at new moon vs sum of apparent radii; coverage % for observer's location.

Planetary occultations — Moon–planet separation scanned every 2 hours vs the Moon's apparent radius. Geocentric, so local visibility is approximate — verify in Stellarium.

Conjunctions

Scanned at 12-hour intervals for all pairs of tracked solar-system objects, refined to ~10 minutes. Moon pairs are shown only within 5°. Daytime conjunctions (neither object above 5° during the night) are hidden by default; toggle them in the legend.

Limitations and Known Accuracy

  • Positions: Moon and most planets ~1′; Jupiter and Saturn up to ~5′ (see the accuracy table above)
  • Conjunction timing: ~10 minutes (12-hour scan, refined); separations are geocentric
  • Eclipse detection: umbral magnitude within ~5%; type correct
  • Galilean moon events: within ~6 minutes of JPL Horizons (see above)
  • Saturn ring tilt: ~0.1° (checked against JPL Horizons)
  • No aberration or nutation — under 0.01°, negligible for planning
  • No atmospheric refraction — add ~0.5° near horizon
  • Mean equinox of date — everything is precessed to the current date (v13)
  • Times use your browser's time zone — if you plan for a location in another time zone, times display in your own zone
  • GRS position — drifts unpredictably; check the current System II longitude in Sky & Telescope a few times a year and override if needed
